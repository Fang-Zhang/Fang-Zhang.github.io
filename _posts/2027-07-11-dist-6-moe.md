## Parameters that do not cost FLOPs, and the price

In a mixture-of-experts layer each token is sent to one expert MLP chosen by a learned router, so parameters grow with the number of experts while the compute per token stays at one MLP. The price is paid elsewhere: the tokens must travel to the ranks that hold their experts and back, and the number of tokens each expert receives is decided by the router, not by the schedule. [Part 5](/posts/dist-5-pipeline/) had a bubble that a formula predicted; here the idle time is data dependent, so it has to be measured from real routing.

## What the sources say

[Switch Transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.03961) routes each token to the expert with the highest router probability (top-1) and multiplies the expert output by that probability. Its definitions, as used below:

- **Expert capacity** is (tokens per batch / number of experts) x capacity factor. Tokens beyond an expert's capacity are dropped: their computation is skipped and the representation passes to the next layer through the residual connection. A larger capacity factor reduces overflow but increases computation and communication through padding. The paper reports typically under 1 percent dropped tokens with its load-balancing loss.
- **Auxiliary loss**: N x sum over experts of f_i P_i, scaled by a coefficient alpha, where f_i is the fraction of tokens dispatched to expert i and P_i the mean router probability of expert i. P is differentiable and f is not. The paper uses alpha = 0.01, "sufficiently large to ensure load balancing while small enough to not overwhelm the primary cross-entropy objective". Under uniform routing f_i = P_i = 1/N, so the sum of f_i P_i is 1/N and the scaled value is 1 whatever N is.
- **Communication**: with experts spread over n devices, an all-to-all of size [E, C, d_model] moves the dispatched tokens so that the expert dimension is sharded, and a second all-to-all of the same tensor size returns the results.
- **Reported results**: up to 7x pre-training speedups over T5-Base and T5-Large with the same FLOPs per token, and a model of up to a trillion parameters with a 4x speedup over T5-XXL.

## Experiments

**A. Training with routing statistics.** A small language model on characters (vocabulary 128) over the Python standard-library source files, 4 layers, d = 128, 4 heads. In the MoE variants every layer's MLP is replaced by 8 experts of the same shape (4d hidden) with a bias-free linear router and top-1 routing, capacity factor 1.25 with dropping during training. 800 steps of 32 sequences of 128 characters (4,096 tokens per step), AdamW, two seeds. The balancing coefficient alpha was 0, 0.01 or 0.1, plus a dense baseline. Every 50 steps the number of tokens routed to each expert in each layer was recorded before dropping.

**B. Expert parallelism.** The trained models, 8 experts over n = 2, 4 and 8 ranks (8/n experts per rank), gloo, one thread per rank. The batch of 32 sequences is split over the ranks, the router and the attention are replicated, tokens are sorted by destination, sizes are exchanged with an all-to-all, tokens move with a variable-size `all_to_all_single` wrapped in a custom autograd function whose backward is the reverse all-to-all, and the results come back through a second one. No token is dropped here. The output, loss and all gradients are compared with one process on the whole batch; calls, bytes and tokens processed per rank are counted.

Training results (two seeds; validation bits per character, lower is better):

- **Quality.** Dense: 2.429 and 2.409. MoE with alpha = 0: 2.608 and 2.719. alpha = 0.01: 2.440 and 2.453. alpha = 0.1: 2.407 and 2.449. The MoE has 4,534,656 parameters against 842,624 for the dense model, with about the same compute per token (plus a 4,096-parameter router per layer). At this scale and length the balanced MoE matched the dense model and did not beat it, so no quality advantage is claimed; the unbalanced MoE was clearly worse.
- **Imbalance.** At initialisation the busiest expert received 1.83 times the mean load (min over experts 0.45 times the mean) for all settings, because the router starts random. With alpha = 0 the ratio peaked at 3.02 at step 50 and stayed near 2 (1.98 at step 750), while the least used expert received 0.07 of the mean. With alpha = 0.01 it fell to 1.23 (least used 0.78) and with alpha = 0.1 to 1.11 (0.84) at step 750.
- **Dropped tokens.** The fraction of tokens over capacity, averaged over steps 100 to 750: with alpha = 0, 31.8, 19.8, 10.4 and 2.0 percent at capacity factors 1.0, 1.25, 1.5 and 2.0; with alpha = 0.01, 7.1, 0.8, 0.1 and 0.0 percent; with alpha = 0.1, 4.9, 0.3, 0.0 and 0.0 percent. The capacity factor needed for no drop at the last snapshot averaged 1.98 (max 2.46) at alpha = 0, 1.23 (max 1.30) at 0.01 and 1.11 (max 1.15) at 0.1.

Expert-parallel results (all nine runs, n = 2, 4, 8, three checkpoints):

- **Equivalence.** Maximum logit difference 3.3e-06 (exactly 0 in most runs), replicated-parameter gradients after the sum over ranks within 1.8e-08, and expert gradients within 7.0e-10 of one process; the losses agreed to 5 decimals.
- **Calls.** 16 all-to-alls per rank per step: 4 layers x (dispatch and return, forward and backward).
- **Bytes.** Each all-to-all moves the rank's local tokens times d times 4 bytes in total, of which the share that leaves the rank was close to (n-1)/n (0.48 at n = 2, 0.87 at n = 8). Per rank and step the off-rank bytes were 8.1, 6.2 and 3.6 MB at n = 2, 4 and 8 (alpha = 0; the other checkpoints within 2 percent of these).
- **Straggler.** The busiest rank processed this many times the mean number of tokens, averaged over the 4 layers, at n = 2, 4, 8: 1.11, 1.42, 1.88 with alpha = 0; 1.07, 1.13, 1.21 with alpha = 0.01; 1.04, 1.07, 1.11 with alpha = 0.1.
- **Padding.** A fixed-size all-to-all that never drops must be padded to the busiest (source rank, expert) pair. The capacity factor that does this was 2.10, 2.38 and 2.48 at alpha = 0 (n = 2, 4, 8) and 1.27, 1.59 and 1.98 at alpha = 0.1, so at n = 8 the padded all-to-alls would carry 9.1 MB per rank and step at alpha = 0 and 7.3 MB at alpha = 0.1, against 3.6 MB for the variable-size version that was run (arithmetic from the measured counts, not an additional run).

## Engineering reading

These points are this series' reading of the measurements and the source.

1. **Balance is not a property the router has by default; the auxiliary loss creates it.** The random initial router was already 1.83 times unbalanced, and without the loss training made it worse, driving some experts to 7 percent of the mean load. A coefficient of 0.01, the value the paper uses, removed most of the effect on quality and on drops in this test, and 0.1 did slightly better on both without a measurable quality cost, though two seeds cannot separate 2.428 from 2.447.
2. **Dropping silently turns imbalance into a modelling error.** The unbalanced run lost 19.8 percent of its tokens' MLP computation at the usual capacity factor of 1.25 and ended 0.18 and 0.31 bits per character behind the dense model. This is the mechanism behind the paper's emphasis on keeping drops low. The alternative, raising the capacity factor, pays for the imbalance in compute and bytes: no drop at alpha = 0 needed a factor of about 2, so about twice the expert FLOPs and padded traffic.
3. **Traffic grows with tokens, not with experts.** The bytes per all-to-all depend on local tokens and d only: 8 experts or 800 would move the same tokens. This is what lets parameters scale without a matching increase in per-token compute or traffic, and it is a different accounting from tensor parallelism (Part 4), where the messages are activations of every layer, and from data parallelism (Part 2), where they are gradients of every parameter. Per-rank bytes fall with n (6.2 MB at n = 4, 3.6 MB at n = 8) only because the local batch shrinks as 1/n; the off-rank share stays close to (n-1)/n, so there is no locality gain and the traffic summed over ranks is nearly the same at n = 4 and n = 8.
4. **The slowest rank sets the step.** If expert compute is spread as measured, the busiest rank bounds the layer time, so utilisation of the expert FLOPs is at most 1 / (busiest over mean): 53 percent at n = 8 with alpha = 0, 83 percent at 0.01 and 90 percent at 0.1. The ratio got worse as n grew, plausibly because each rank holds fewer experts and so averages over fewer of them (not isolated here); this is the data-dependent analogue of the pipeline bubble.
5. **Fewer tokens per rank make routing noisier.** The no-drop capacity factor measured per source rank grew from 1.27 at n = 2 to 1.98 at n = 8 at alpha = 0.1, while the global ratio was 1.11. With small per-rank batches the busiest (rank, expert) pair deviates more from the mean, so the padded cost depends on the parallel layout as well as on the router.

## Limits

- The data is Python source as characters, the model has 4.5M parameters and trains for 800 steps; the routing behaviour of production models trained for much longer, on mixed text, with specialised experts, was not measured, and the result about quality compares one dense model with MoE variants at equal steps, not at equal loss or equal compute.
- Two seeds per training configuration; the expert-parallel runs used the seed-1 checkpoints only, with one batch of 32 sequences, so straggler ratios are single-batch values.
- Routing statistics are the pre-dropping counts per batch of 4,096 tokens; the capacity-overflow percentages for factors other than 1.25 are computed from those counts after the fact, not from separate training runs.
- Expert parallelism was run without dropping and with the auxiliary loss switched off in the comparison; the data-parallel all-reduce of replicated parameters is part of the equivalence check but is not included in the all-to-all byte counts.
- Ranks are processes on one CPU host over loopback sockets, so no timing is reported; only counts, bytes, token ratios and equivalence carry over to GPU clusters. Padded sizes are computed, not run, and no capacity-padded implementation was tested.

## Code and output

Layers and autograd all-to-all:

```python
import math, torch, torch.nn as nn, torch.nn.functional as F, torch.distributed as dist, lm
CN = {"a2a_calls": 0, "a2a_bytes_total": 0, "a2a_bytes_offrank": 0}
class A2A(torch.autograd.Function):
    @staticmethod
    def forward(ctx, x, in_splits, out_splits, rank):
        ctx.s = (in_splits, out_splits, rank); out = x.new_empty(sum(out_splits), x.shape[1]); dist.all_to_all_single(out, x.contiguous(), out_splits, in_splits)
        CN["a2a_calls"] += 1; CN["a2a_bytes_total"] += sum(in_splits) * x.shape[1] * 4; CN["a2a_bytes_offrank"] += (sum(in_splits) - in_splits[rank]) * x.shape[1] * 4; return out
    @staticmethod
    def backward(ctx, g):
        in_s, out_s, rank = ctx.s; gi = g.new_empty(sum(in_s), g.shape[1]); dist.all_to_all_single(gi, g.contiguous(), in_s, out_s)
        CN["a2a_calls"] += 1; CN["a2a_bytes_total"] += sum(out_s) * g.shape[1] * 4; CN["a2a_bytes_offrank"] += (sum(out_s) - out_s[rank]) * g.shape[1] * 4; return gi, None, None, None
class MoE(nn.Module):
    def __init__(s, D, E, cf, alpha):
        super().__init__(); s.E, s.cf, s.alpha = E, cf, alpha; s.r = nn.Linear(D, E, bias=False); s.ex = nn.ModuleList(nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(D, 4 * D), nn.GELU(), nn.Linear(4 * D, D)) for _ in range(E)); s.ep = None; s.aux = 0.0; s.counts = None; s.dropped = 0; s.recv_counts = None
    def forward(s, x):
        B, T_, D = x.shape; x = x.reshape(-1, D); T = x.shape[0]; p = F.softmax(s.r(x), -1); g, idx = p.max(-1); counts = torch.bincount(idx, minlength=s.E); s.counts = counts.detach()
        s.aux = s.alpha * s.E * ((counts / T).detach() * p.mean(0)).sum()
        if s.ep is None:
            keep = torch.ones(T, dtype=torch.bool)
            if s.cf:
                cap = math.ceil(s.cf * T / s.E); order = idx.argsort(stable=True); starts = counts.cumsum(0) - counts; pos = torch.arange(T) - starts[idx[order]]; keep = torch.empty(T, dtype=torch.bool); keep[order] = pos < cap
            s.dropped = int((~keep).sum()); out = torch.zeros_like(x)
            for e in range(s.E):
                ids = ((idx == e) & keep).nonzero().squeeze(1)
                if len(ids): out = out.index_add(0, ids, s.ex[e](x[ids]) * g[ids, None])
            return out.view(B, T_, D)
        n, r = s.ep; le = s.E // n; order = idx.argsort(stable=True); xs = x[order]; cm = counts.view(n, le).contiguous(); in_splits = cm.sum(1).tolist(); rc = torch.empty_like(cm); dist.all_to_all_single(rc, cm); s.recv_counts = rc; out_splits = rc.sum(1).tolist()
        recv = A2A.apply(xs, in_splits, out_splits, r); pieces = []; off = 0
        for src in range(n):
            for j in range(le): c = int(rc[src, j]); pieces.append(s.ex[r * le + j](recv[off:off + c])); off += c
        back = A2A.apply(torch.cat(pieces), out_splits, in_splits, r); return torch.zeros_like(x).index_add(0, order, back * g[order, None]).view(B, T_, D)
class MoEGPT(lm.GPT):
    def __init__(s, D=128, H=4, L=4, E=8, cf=1.25, alpha=0.01):
        super().__init__(D, H, L)
        for b in s.b: b.m = MoE(D, E, cf, alpha)
    def moes(s): return [b.m for b in s.b]
    def aux(s): return sum(m.aux for m in s.moes())
```

Training:

```python
import sys, json, math, time, torch, torch.nn.functional as F, lm, moe
name, alpha, seed = sys.argv[1], sys.argv[2], int(sys.argv[3]); STEPS, B, LR, EV = 800, 32, 2e-3, 50
torch.set_num_threads(4); C = lm.load_code(); ctr, cva = lm.split(C); src = lm.enc("".join(ctr)); val = lm.enc("".join(cva)); torch.manual_seed(seed); g = torch.Generator().manual_seed(seed)
m = lm.GPT() if alpha == "dense" else moe.MoEGPT(alpha=float(alpha)); opt = torch.optim.AdamW(m.parameters(), LR, weight_decay=0.01); probs = torch.ones(1); log = {"name": name, "alpha": alpha, "seed": seed, "loss": [], "counts": [], "dropped": [], "params": sum(q.numel() for q in m.parameters())}; t0 = time.time()
for t in range(STEPS):
    for gr in opt.param_groups: gr["lr"] = LR * min(1, (t + 1) / 100) * (0.1 + 0.9 * 0.5 * (1 + math.cos(math.pi * t / STEPS)))
    x, y = lm.batch([src], probs, B, g); loss = F.cross_entropy(m(x).reshape(-1, lm.V), y.reshape(-1)); tot = loss + (m.aux() if alpha != "dense" else 0); opt.zero_grad(); tot.backward(); opt.step(); log["loss"].append(loss.item())
    if alpha != "dense" and t % EV == 0: log["counts"].append([mo.counts.tolist() for mo in m.moes()]); log["dropped"].append([mo.dropped for mo in m.moes()])
log["val_bpc"] = lm.evaluate(m, val); log["secs"] = time.time() - t0; json.dump(log, open(f"g6_{name}.json", "w")); torch.save(m.state_dict(), f"g6_{name}.pt"); print(name, log["val_bpc"], log["secs"])
```

Expert-parallel check:

```python
import os, sys, json, math, torch, torch.distributed as dist, torch.multiprocessing as mp, torch.nn.functional as F, lm, moe
TOK, E, D = 32, 8, 128
def worker(rank, n, port, ck, outfile):
    os.environ.update(MASTER_ADDR="127.0.0.1", MASTER_PORT=str(port)); torch.set_num_threads(1); dist.init_process_group("gloo", rank=rank, world_size=n)
    sd = torch.load(f"g6_{ck}.pt"); ref = moe.MoEGPT(cf=None, alpha=0.0); ref.load_state_dict(sd); ep = moe.MoEGPT(cf=None, alpha=0.0); ep.load_state_dict(sd)
    for mo in ep.moes(): mo.ep = (n, rank)
    C = lm.load_code(); _, cva = lm.split(C); val = lm.enc("".join(cva)); g = torch.Generator().manual_seed(5); x, y = lm.batch([val], torch.ones(1), TOK, g)
    lg = ref(x); lref = F.cross_entropy(lg.reshape(-1, lm.V), y.reshape(-1)); lref.backward(); xs, ys = x.chunk(n)[rank], y.chunk(n)[rank]
    for k in moe.CN: moe.CN[k] = 0
    out = ep(xs); loss = F.cross_entropy(out.reshape(-1, lm.V), ys.reshape(-1)) / n; loss.backward(); lgd = (out - lg.detach().chunk(n)[rank]).abs().max().item()
    rep = [(nm, q) for nm, q in ep.named_parameters() if ".m.ex." not in nm]; flat = torch.cat([q.grad.flatten() for _, q in rep]); dist.all_reduce(flat); off = 0; gd = 0.0; rp = dict(ref.named_parameters())
    for nm, q in rep: gd = max(gd, (flat[off:off + q.numel()].view_as(q) - rp[nm].grad).abs().max().item()); off += q.numel()
    le = E // n; ed = 0.0
    for nm, q in ep.named_parameters():
        if ".m.ex." in nm and int(nm.split(".m.ex.")[1].split(".")[0]) // le == rank: ed = max(ed, ((q.grad if q.grad is not None else torch.zeros_like(q)) - (rp[nm].grad if rp[nm].grad is not None else torch.zeros_like(q))).abs().max().item())
    info = {"rank": rank, "logit_diff": lgd, "loss_ref": lref.item(), "loss_part": loss.item() * n, "rep_grad_diff": gd, "expert_grad_diff": ed, "cn": dict(moe.CN), "local_counts": [mo.counts.tolist() for mo in ep.moes()], "processed": [int(mo.recv_counts.sum()) for mo in ep.moes()], "Tr": xs.shape[0] * xs.shape[1]}
    allr = [None] * n; dist.all_gather_object(allr, info)
    if rank == 0: json.dump({"ck": ck, "n": n, "ranks": allr}, open(outfile, "w"))
    dist.destroy_process_group()
if __name__ == "__main__":
    for ck in sys.argv[1:]:
        for n in (2, 4, 8): mp.spawn(worker, args=(n, 30200 + n, ck, f"g6_ep_{ck}_{n}.json"), nprocs=n)
```
