## Two kinds of race

The split used here is my own, and it is by where the race happens: near a shore, or offshore. It matters because the two kinds ask different questions. Near the shore, the race is short, spectators can see it, and the decisions come every few seconds. Offshore, the race lasts days or months, nobody sees most of it, and the decisions are about weather and endurance.

## Near the shore

**America's Cup.** The oldest of them. The boat is the AC75, a foiling monohull sailed by five people, with the trimming functions [powered by battery](https://www.americascup.com/the-boats). The Cup is a challenge event: the winner of a challenger selection series, the Louis Vuitton Cup, then [races the defender in a head-to-head Match](https://www.americascup.com/38th-americascup). The 38th edition is [planned for Naples in summer 2027](https://www.sailing.org/2025/08/12/the-louis-vuitton-38th-americas-cup-protocol-reveals-a-groundbreaking-new-partnership-for-the-long-term-future-of-sports-oldest-international-trophy), under a new protocol that also aims to make the event [recur every two years](https://www.sailing.org/2025/08/12/the-louis-vuitton-38th-americas-cup-protocol-reveals-a-groundbreaking-new-partnership-for-the-long-term-future-of-sports-oldest-international-trophy). INEOS Britannia recorded [55.6 knots](https://www.americascup.com/the-boats) in the 37th edition's challenger final.

**SailGP.** A league rather than a one-off event. Every team sails the same boat, the [F50, a 15-metre foiling catamaran](https://sailgp.com/news/24/beginners-guide-f50-foiling-catamaran/), so the crew is the only difference. According to the league's [2026 season guide](https://www.ussailgpteam.com/articles/92400abb-b0ad-4d0b-ad47-07842412e406/how-does-the-sailgp-season-work-how-do-teams-win-sailgp), 13 teams race at 13 events. Each event has seven fleet races of at most 16 minutes, then a final. Fleet-race points run from 10 for first place down to 1 for tenth, and 0 below that. The F50 speed record is [103.93 km/h](https://sailgp.com/news/24/beginners-guide-f50-foiling-catamaran/).

**The Olympics.** There are [10 sailing events at Paris 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sailing_at_the_2024_Summer_Olympics): four for men, four for women and two mixed. Each event uses one class of boat, so the equipment is the same for everyone. The classes include the ILCA 7 and ILCA 6 single-handed dinghies, the 470, the Nacra 17 foiling catamaran, the 49er and 49erFX skiffs, and windsurfing and kite foiling.

## Offshore

**Vendée Globe.** Solo, non-stop and [without assistance](https://www.vendeeglobe.org/en/what-vendee-globe), round the world in IMOCA 60 boats, starting and finishing at Les Sables-d'Olonne in France. The course is [about 24,000 nautical miles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vend%C3%A9e_Globe). In the first edition in 1989, thirteen sailors started and [seven finished](https://www.vendeeglobe.org/en/what-vendee-globe). The record belongs to Charlie Dalin: [64 days, 19 hours, 22 minutes and 49 seconds](https://www.imoca.org/en/news/news/charlie-dalin-wins-the-2024-vendee-globe) in the 2024-25 race, which started with 40 skippers.

**The Ocean Race.** A crewed round-the-world race, held [every three or four years since 1973](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ocean_Race) and originally called the Whitbread. It has used IMOCA 60 boats since 2021, and the 2023 edition ran 10 legs with a second class of Volvo Ocean 65 boats. Its [notice of race](https://theoceanrace.s3.amazonaws.com/files/m146409_The-Ocean-Race-NOR-2022-23_-Amendment-6.pdf) also awards points for short in-port races held at the stopovers.

**Rolex Sydney Hobart.** A [628 nautical mile](https://images.cycaracing.com/media/3452192/2026-rshyr-nor-final-final.pdf) passage race from Sydney to Hobart, started on Boxing Day (26 December) since 1945. Boats of very different sizes enter, so the overall winner is decided on [IRC corrected time](https://images.cycaracing.com/media/3452192/2026-rshyr-nor-final-final.pdf), a handicap that adjusts each boat's time for its rating. The first boat across the line wins line honours, a separate prize.

**Rolex Fastnet Race.** Held every two years from Cowes on the English south coast, around the Fastnet Rock off southern Ireland, and since 2021 finishing at [Cherbourg in France, about 695 nautical miles in total](https://www.rolexfastnetrace.com/en/competitors/race-documents/distances-and-course). Before 2021 it finished at Plymouth on a [608 nautical mile course](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fastnet_Race).

## What the races have in common

Three observations, and the second and third are my own reading, not something the organisers state.

1. **Winning is defined differently each time.** Head-to-head wins, points per place, elapsed time, corrected time. A sailor needs the scoring rule before the tactics make sense, which is what the next post covers.
2. **Equal boats move the contest to the crew.** The F50, the Olympic classes and the AC75's one-design core all limit what money can buy. Where boats differ, as at Sydney Hobart, a handicap does the equalising instead.
3. **Every one of them runs on measured numbers.** Wind speed and direction, boat speed, heading, position, depth. The 15-metre boat and the solo boat read the same kinds of quantities, only with different consequences for a mistake.
