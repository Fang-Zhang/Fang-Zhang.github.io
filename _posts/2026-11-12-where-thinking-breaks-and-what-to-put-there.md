This series treats clear thinking as a practice with known failure points. Thinking does not break at random. A mind with a small working memory, a pull toward the easiest option, a habit of mistaking its models for the world, poor instincts about size and odds, and a new habit of asking machines that tend to agree, fails in the same places again and again.

Each post takes one failure point, shows the evidence for it from the original studies or sources, and offers one safeguard. A safeguard here means a decision made before the moment of failure, while thinking is still clear. Odysseus did not resist the Sirens by willpower; he had himself tied to the mast beforehand. The rest of the series is that move, applied six ways.

## The map

The stages run from the inside out: the mind, the setting around it, the beliefs it holds, the estimates it makes, the direction it chooses, and the tools it hands work to.

## How each post works

Each post opens by saying where it sits on the map. Claims come from named sources, read in the original wherever possible. Where a result reaches the post only through a secondary source, the post says so. Where an argument or a simulation is the author's own, the post marks it as such.
