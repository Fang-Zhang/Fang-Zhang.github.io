Retail and logistics employ people in the hundreds of thousands per company, mostly away from a desk. When a firm of that size announces AI training, the design choices are visible even if the results are not. This part reads two primary announcements, Walmart's note to its U.S. associates and FedEx's press release, and records what each says. Both are announcements of programmes that had just launched or were about to; neither reports outcomes, and this post does not supply any.

## Walmart: certification through the existing academy

Source: [the note from Walmart's Chief People Officer to U.S. associates](https://corporate.walmart.com/news/2025/09/04/donna-morris-to-associates-being-people-led-means-investing-in-you), published on 4 September 2025.

- **Audience.** "U.S. frontline and office-based associates", beginning in 2026.
- **Format.** A new OpenAI Certifications programme, for which Walmart is "working with them to create a customized experience". Associates get "free access to a tailored version of this certification" through Walmart Academy, which the note describes as "the largest private training program in the world with over 3.5 million participants".
- **Framing.** The training is "designed to help you at work and in your personal life in an increasingly digital world", and sits on top of a "nearly $1 billion commitment to skills training through 2026". Until the certification launches, associates can use existing AI training through the Live Better education benefit.
- **What is absent.** The note gives no curriculum, no completion target and no measure of use on the job. The 3.5 million refers to Academy participants across all topics, not to AI training.

## FedEx: enterprise-wide, role-based, with a consulting partner

Source: [FedEx's press release](https://newsroom.fedex.com/newsroom/global-english/fedex-empowers-global-workforce-with-ai-education-and-literacy-program), 2 December 2025, and [CNBC's report on the rollout](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/21/fedex-jobs-ai-training.html), whose headline states that FedEx has started delivering the training to over 400,000 workers.

- **Audience.** An "enterprise-wide AI Education and Literacy program" with "personalized, roles-based trainings for team members around the world".
- **Format.** FedEx builds internal training that "expand[s] on AI fundamentals" and has engaged Accenture to supply customised programmes through its LearnVantage platform: "tailored, scalable training, complete with skill recognition and interactive live training sessions". Personalised pathways depend on where a person is in their career.
- **Stated purpose.** "A shared language of technology understanding", with employees learning "to apply AI responsibly within their function". Leaders are to be "equipped with the knowledge of how to embed AI into their own work, their teams' work, and cross-functional processes".
- **What is absent.** The release names no completion rate, no function-level curriculum and no result. Only the CNBC headline gives a count, and only the headline was available for this post.

## Reading across

These are the author's observations, not claims from the sources.

- **Both firms use an outside certifier and an existing delivery channel.** Walmart pairs an AI lab's certification with its own academy; FedEx pairs a consulting firm's platform with internal modules. Neither announcement describes a curriculum written specifically for store or hub work.
- **"Role-based" is the word both lean on.** Walmart says "customized"; FedEx says "roles-based". Neither announcement shows what differs between a store associate's and a hub worker's path, so the claim cannot be assessed.
- **Scale is stated as reach, not as use.** Part 6 cites a survey where frontline employees reported far less upskilling than leaders. These announcements address the same population; whether they close that gap is not answered by them.
- **The announcements pre-date the evidence.** The UK programme in Parts 1 to 5 reports 10 case studies with self-reported outcomes because it looked at programmes already running. A press release at launch cannot.

## Limits

- Two announcements, two companies, no outcomes. This part describes plans, not results.
- The FedEx headcount comes from a headline, and the Walmart figure of 3.5 million is for the whole Academy.
- Walmart and FedEx are among the largest employers in their sectors; small retailers and carriers, which the UK analysis describes as lacking training capacity, are not represented.
- Both documents are company communications and are written to promote the programme.
