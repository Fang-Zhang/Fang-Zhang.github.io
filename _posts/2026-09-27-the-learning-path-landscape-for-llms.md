Learning large language models isn't one long staircase — it's a series of distinct stages, each with its own state of mind and its own best resources. Here's a map of that path.

| Stage | Learner's State | Representative Solutions |
|---|---|---|
| Foundations | Needs Python, linear algebra, calculus, statistics | [DataCamp](https://www.datacamp.com/), [Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org/), general CS coursework |
| Theoretical Framework | Systematically learning core ML/DL concepts | [Andrew Ng's ML/DL Specializations](https://www.coursera.org/specializations/deep-learning) (4.8M+ students), [StatQuest](https://www.youtube.com/@statquest) |
| Architectural Intuition | Understanding *why* Transformers/LLMs are designed the way they are | [Jay Alammar](https://jalammar.github.io/), [3Blue1Brown](https://www.youtube.com/@3blue1brown), StatQuest |
| Following Along, Building | Coding along with a video/book, implementing GPT/Transformer from scratch | [Andrej Karpathy — Zero to Hero](https://karpathy.ai/zero-to-hero.html), [Sebastian Raschka — Build a Large Language Model from Scratch](https://www.manning.com/books/build-a-large-language-model-from-scratch), [Umar Jamil](https://www.youtube.com/@umarjamilai), Vizuara, [CodeEmporium](https://www.youtube.com/@CodeEmporium) |
| Engineering at Scale | Handling real engineering problems — out-of-memory errors, loss not converging, distributed training | HuggingFace/PyTorch forums, [Trelis Research](https://www.youtube.com/@TrelisResearch), Abhishek Thakur, [Sam Witteveen](https://www.youtube.com/@samwitteveenai), [James Briggs](https://www.youtube.com/@jamesbriggs), Unsloth/Daniel Han, AI Anytime |
| Frontier / Research Level | Reproducing papers, participating in research | Original papers, [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/), conference talks, [Karpathy's nanoGPT](https://github.com/karpathy/nanoGPT), [Yannic Kilcher](https://www.youtube.com/@YannicKilcher), AI Coffee Break with Letitia, 跟李沐学AI, [Machine Learning Street Talk](https://www.youtube.com/@MachineLearningStreetTalk), The AI Epiphany, [Two Minute Papers](https://www.youtube.com/@TwoMinutePapers), Stanford CS25: Transformers United |

What strikes me most about this map isn't any single resource — it's how densely covered most of the path is. Foundations through "follow-along implementation" is a well-trodden highway, competed over by some of the best educators in the field. Even the harder, messier engineering-at-scale and research-level stages have real, if more scattered, communities and content.
