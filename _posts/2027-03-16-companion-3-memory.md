[Part 1](/posts/companion-1-requirements/) showed by arithmetic that a companion's history outgrows any context window. This part measures the choice that follows. A synthetic user talks to an assistant over 10 or 30 sessions; one question per fact is asked at the end; three memory strategies supply the context. The results are accuracy, prompt tokens and seconds per answer on CPU.

## What the literature says

- [LongMemEval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10813) evaluates five memory abilities: information extraction, multi-session reasoning, temporal reasoning, knowledge updates and abstention. It reports a 30% accuracy drop for commercial chat assistants and long-context LLMs on memorizing information across sustained interactions, and splits memory design into indexing, retrieval and reading. Its optimisations are session decomposition, fact-augmented key expansion and time-aware query expansion.
- [LoCoMo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17753) finds that long-context models and retrieval augmentation offer improvements but still lag well behind human performance.
- [MemGPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08560) manages a limited window like an operating system manages memory, paging between tiers.
- [RAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401) combines a parametric model with non-parametric memory accessed by a retriever.

Two of the LongMemEval categories, extraction and knowledge updates, are the ones built into the test below.

## Setup

Everything is generated by a script with a fixed seed, so the facts are known and no real conversation is involved.

- A history is S sessions of 8 user turns, each followed by an assistant reply, dated 3 days apart. Most turns are filler ("The weather has been strange all week."). Facts are single filler-style turns placed in random sessions.
- Five facts per user: dog's name, sister's name, favourite food, and two that change. City: "I live in X these days." early, then "We moved to Y last week." late. Hobby: "My hobby is X." early, then "I gave up X and my hobby is now Y." late.
- Each fact has a distractor about someone else: a neighbour's dog, a cousin, a colleague's favourite food, a friend's city.
- Questions: "What is my dog's name?", "Which city do I live in now?", and so on. An answer counts if it contains the gold value. Greedy decoding.
- Sizes: 10 sessions (about 2.9K prompt tokens, 4 users, 20 questions) and 30 sessions (about 8.5K tokens, 2 users, 10 questions). Model: Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, bfloat16, 16 CPU threads.

Three strategies:

- `full`: the whole history in the prompt.
- `bm25`: one chunk per user turn plus its reply, stamped with the session date; the four top-scoring chunks go into the prompt in date order.
- `recent`: the last 20 turns, about 760 tokens.

The retriever is plain BM25, which scores a chunk $d$ for a query $q$ as

$$\text{score}(q,d)=\sum_{w\in q}\log\!\Big(1+\frac{N-n_w+0.5}{n_w+0.5}\Big)\frac{f_{w,d}\,(k_1+1)}{f_{w,d}+k_1\big(1-b+b\,|d|/\overline{|d|}\big)}$$

with $N$ chunks, $n_w$ chunks containing the word $w$, $f_{w,d}$ the count in the chunk, $k_1=1.5$ and $b=0.75$.

```python
def bm25(query, docs, k=4, k1=1.5, b=0.75):
    toks = [W(d[1]) for d in docs]; N = len(docs); avg = sum(map(len, toks)) / N
    df = Counter(w for t in toks for w in set(t)); sc = []
    for t in toks:
        tf = Counter(t); s = 0
        for w in W(query):
            if w in tf: s += math.log(1 + (N - df[w] + .5) / (df[w] + .5)) * tf[w] * (k1 + 1) / (tf[w] + k1 * (1 - b + b * len(t) / avg))
        sc.append(s)
    return [docs[i][1] for i in sorted(sorted(range(N), key=lambda i: -sc[i])[:k])]   # top k, back in date order
```

## Results

*Left: accuracy by strategy and size. Middle: by question type, both sizes pooled. Right: mean prompt tokens against mean seconds per answer, from 10 sessions to 30.*

| Strategy | 10 sessions: accuracy, tokens, s per answer | 30 sessions: accuracy, tokens, s per answer |
|---|---|---|
| full history | 16/20, 2,859, 72.7 | 8/10, 8,508, 296.5 |
| BM25 top-4 | 16/20, 193, 5.6 | 8/10, 196, 5.9 |
| last 20 turns | 6/20, 761, 19.1 | 1/10, 763, 19.6 |

- Full history and BM25 tie on accuracy at both sizes. BM25 gets there with about 200 prompt tokens, which is 1/15 of the full prompt at 10 sessions and 1/43 at 30, and with 13× and 50× less time per answer on this machine.
- The recent window fails once the fact falls outside it: 5 of 18 one-time facts and 2 of 12 updated ones.
- Cost grows with history only for `full`. Seconds per token are about 25 ms at 2.9K tokens and 35 ms at 8.5K, so cost grows slightly faster than linearly here.

The question types separate the strategies from the tie. One-time facts: 18 of 18 for both `full` and `bm25`. Facts that were later updated: 6 of 12 for each, split by fact:

- Hobby ("my hobby is now Y"): 6 of 6 for both.
- City: 0 of 6 for both. Every wrong answer is the earlier city, never the friend's.

The two strategies fail the city question for different reasons.

- `bm25`: the update turn "We moved to Y last week" shares no word with "Which city do I live in now?", so it is not retrieved. Recall of the gold-value turn in the top 4 (and top 8) is 4 of 8 at 10 sessions and 2 of 4 at 30 for update questions, against 18 of 18 for one-time facts. Each retrieval miss is a wrong answer.
- `full`: the update is in the prompt, and the model still answers with the earlier city. Both mentions are present and it picks the wrong one.

## Reading the results

- On extraction, retrieval costs nothing in accuracy here and saves most of the compute. This agrees with the direction of the cost arithmetic in Part 1, and the 30-session prompt is still small next to a months-long history.
- An update is a different problem from an extraction: the answer depends on which of two statements is newer and on whether the retriever can find the newer one when the question shares no words with it. LongMemEval's key expansion and time-aware query expansion target the indexing and query side of this. They were not tested here.
- The hobby update is stated as "my hobby is now", which repeats the query's words, and it passes everywhere. The city update was written without them, and it fails everywhere. The wording of the stored fact, not the strategy, separates the two here.

## Limits

- Synthetic, templated histories with one fact per turn: real conversations spread a fact over several turns and say it in many forms. The retriever is lexical, and a dense retriever might find "We moved to Y" from the question; this was not tested.
- 30 questions in total, 10 at the larger size. Differences of one or two answers are not meaningful, so only the large gaps (the recent window, the cost columns) and the pattern by question type are reported.
- Answers were first cut at 12 generated tokens. Three of the `full` answers were truncated mid-sentence before the value ("Based on the conversation, your favorite food appears to be"), so they were regenerated with 60 tokens. Two were then correct and are counted so. The other strategies' answers were short and untouched.
- One 1.5B model, greedy decoding. Larger models handle long context better, and the 8.5K-token prompt is far below real histories. Timings are for this CPU and an unoptimised bfloat16 path; absolute values will differ on other hardware, and a prefix cache would change the `full` row.
- Summaries and structured fact stores (MemGPT-style tiers, extracted facts with timestamps) were not tested, and the test has no abstention and no multi-session reasoning questions.
