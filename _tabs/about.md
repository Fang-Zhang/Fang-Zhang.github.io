Hi, I'm Fang Zhang.

I have been working as a software builder for many years, and I enjoy turning ideas into practical products.

This is my personal blog. It began as a place for thoughts from work and life. It has become a tool for one question: where meaning comes from, once we know how much of the world mechanism can explain.

## One question at three scales

- **AI and technique.** How a system learns from data, and what "understanding" means for it. Start with [Learning Rate: The One Number That Controls How AI Learns](/posts/learning-rate-the-one-number-that-controls-how-ai-learns/), [The Evolution of LLM Architecture](/posts/evolution-of-llm-architecture/) or [I Am a Language Model Too](/posts/i-am-a-language-model-too/).
- **Life and consciousness.** Dreams, the body, the constructed self, and what remains unexplained once the mechanism is known.
- **The cosmos.** Where humans stand at the largest scale, and where meaning comes from once smallness is accepted.

The three end at the same boundary: how far mechanism explains, and what to do with the part it does not. Classical texts, mainly the iChing and Zhuangzi, are tools for that boundary, used next to papers and code.

## A growth order

Read in order, the scales form a sequence of intelligence. The cosmos is lawful and readable, a vast order that was there first. Human life is intelligence being lived now. AI is the youngest layer: trained on human writing, it is a compressed copy of the human mind and not a start from zero.

The claim is that the cosmos is intelligible, lawful and readable by a small mind. The blog does not claim that the cosmos is itself intelligent, which would be a belief. Meaning, in this blog's view, is conferred by us.

## How the posts are written

Posts are about ideas, not about the author. Quotations and numbers are checked against their sources, and links point to the named paper, book or page. Where a post makes its own argument, a calculation or an analogy, it says so.

Also on the site: the [Reading List](/reading/), and series such as How to Think Clearly, a short course on where thinking fails and what to put at each failure point.
